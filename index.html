FIT2179 - Data Visualisation 2

Migration
to Australia

Net overseas migration is the gap between long-term arrivals and long-term departures. It is the single largest driver of Australia's population growth, outweighing natural increase in most years since 2006. This page follows twenty years of that flow: how the national totals moved, which countries migrants came from, which states received them, and which visas they arrived on.

Part 1

The National Picture

Net overseas migration to Australia stayed between 140,000 and 300,000 people a year around the 2000s and 2010s. COVID border closures then pushed it below zero for the first time, and the rebound drove it to a record 538,000. The three charts in this section show the national total, the visa categories that make it up, and which visa types drove the most recent year.

Takeaway: The 2020-21 collapse and the 2022-23 record peak are the largest swings in twenty years. The rebound did not just bring migration back to normal - it went well past every previous high, mostly because of a big jump in temporary-visa arrivals.
Chart 1

Annual net overseas migration

Net overseas migration is arrivals minus departures - positive means more people arriving than leaving. Australia held steady for two decades before COVID forced the first negative year, then rebounded to a record high.

Chart 2

Arrivals by visa category

Total long-term arrivals broken into four groups: temporary visas, permanent visas, New Zealand citizens, and Australian residents returning. Temporary visa arrivals account for almost all the growth over twenty years.

Chart 3

Net flow by visa class

Each bar is one visa class - arrivals minus departures for the year. Higher-education students alone account for nearly a third of Australia's net migration, while departing Australian citizens are the largest single drag, showing migration is driven by a few key visa categories rather than spread evenly.

Part 1 Conclusion

The three charts together show that Australia's headline migration figure is mostly a temporary-visa story. Permanent migration has barely changed in twenty years. Every spike, dip, and record year comes from short-term visas, which can be changed by government policy at any time. Higher-education students alone make up nearly a third of net migration, which means a small number of policy decisions shape the national figure more than any long-term population trend.

Part 2

Where Migrants Came From

In 2004-05, the United Kingdom was the largest single source of net migration to Australia. By 2024-25, India contributed more than three times as many migrants as the UK. The four charts in this section show that shift at four scales: by region of origin over time, by country rank year on year, as a heatmap across two decades, and as pre- versus post-pandemic averages.

Takeaway: India is now the largest source country in every state except Western Australia. Combined with Nepal, China, and the Philippines, Asia accounts for the majority of Australia's net migration intake in 2024-25.
Chart 4

Net migration by region of origin

Annual net migration grouped into nine world regions. Southern and Central Asia overtakes North-West Europe as the largest source around 2007, and the gap has widened almost every year since.

Chart 5

Annual rank of the top six source countries

Each line follows one country across twenty years. Crossings mark the year one country overtook another. India holds rank one for most of the period from 2013 onward.

Chart 6

Country by year heatmap of net migration

Eight major source countries across twenty-one years. Red cells show net inflows, blue cells show outflows. The blue column in 2020-21 is the COVID border closure, when almost every country sent more people out than in.

Chart 7

Average net migration, pre- and post-pandemic

Average annual net migration before COVID compared with after. Steeper upward slopes mark the countries whose contribution grew the most - China nearly tripled its annual intake, while the UK and South Africa barely moved.

PRE-COVID vs POST-COVID

The slope chart compares two three-year averages instead of two single years, so the comparison is not distorted by COVID. The baseline is 2017-18 to 2019-20. The post-pandemic figure is 2022-23 to 2024-25. India's annual average rose by 11,000 between the two periods. China's rose by 31,000. The Philippines roughly doubled. The UK and South Africa barely moved. The steepness of each line shows how much each country's contribution grew structurally, with the pandemic year itself removed from view.

Part 2 Conclusion

Australia's migration intake has shifted from Europe to Asia. Chart 4 shows Southern and Central Asia overtaking North-West Europe around 2007. Chart 5 has India at rank one from 2013 onward, with the UK falling out of the top three by 2014. Chart 6 records COVID as a column of negative flows in 2020-21 affecting every major source. Chart 7 confirms the rebound is structural, not temporary: India, China, and the Philippines all sit at higher three-year averages than before the pandemic.

Part 3

Where Migrants Settled

National totals hide local variation. Each state and territory has its own migration profile, shaped by labour markets, universities, long-standing ties, and the visa types that bring people in. The charts in this section show migration by state in raw numbers, per resident, and by country of origin.

Takeaway: Raw counts put NSW and Victoria on top. Per resident, the Northern Territory leads - at three times Tasmania's rate. The two views answer different questions about the same flow.
Chart 8

Net overseas migration per 1,000 residents, by state

Migration totals divided by each state's population, so big and small states can be compared fairly. The Northern Territory tops the country with 14 migrants per 1,000 residents - roughly three times Tasmania's rate, even though its raw count is one of the lowest.

Why per-capita matters? NSW and Victoria look biggest in raw numbers simply because more people live there. Dividing by population shows which states are actually being changed the most by migration, relative to the people already living there.
Chart 9

Top three source countries per state.

Each state's leading sources. NSW and VIC draw most heavily from India and China; WA is the only state where the UK still leads.

Why India Dominates

Indian migration to Australia is dominated by higher-education students, who concentrate in the same cities where Indian community networks are largest. NSW, Victoria, and Queensland together host more than 70% of Australia's international student enrolments, which is why India tops their rankings.

Two outliers worth noticing

Western Australia is the only state where the UK still leads. The link goes back to postwar migration schemes that filled British workers into Perth's trades and mining. Queensland is the only state where New Zealand leads, a function of the trans-Tasman free-movement arrangement and the state's climate and cost advantages.

What chart 9 tells us

The top-three rankings differ from state to state, but the underlying truth is consistent: India, China, and one historic source (UK or New Zealand) appear in almost every list. Australia's migration system is less regionally varied than it first appears. The same countries dominate everywhere.

Chart 10

Regional composition of net migration by state

Each bar shows one state's net migration broken down by region of origin and normalised to 100%. Small multiples allow composition to be compared across states without losing the part-to-whole detail.

Part 3 Conclusion

The map gives geography, the ranked bars give precision, the small multiples give composition. The three views disagree usefully. NSW and Victoria dominate raw counts but sit mid-table per capita. The Northern Territory ranks last by raw count but first per resident. Each state draws on a different mix of source countries, and that mix is not random - Western Australia retains its British connection, Queensland retains its New Zealand one, NSW and Victoria pull most heavily from India and China. The national headline figure is the sum of eight different migration systems sharing one border.

Part 4

Which Visas Brought Them

Visa categories settle very unevenly across Australia. Students, working-holiday makers, skilled workers, and New Zealand citizens each cluster in different states. This section looks at which visa class drives each state, and how the student-visa story changed over twenty years.

Takeaway: Higher-education students are the biggest pull factor for New South Wales and Victoria. Queensland is held up by New Zealand citizens, and Western Australia by skilled workers. No single visa class dominates the country - each state has its own.
Chart 11

Net flow by state and visa class

Each column is a state or territory; each row a visa class. Cells are coloured by net flow: red for positive, blue for negative. The brightest red cells are higher-education students concentrating in NSW and Victoria. The brightest blue cells are Australian citizens departing the same states.

Chart 12

Net student-visa flow by state, over time

Each line tracks one state's net international-student visa migration. The pattern is national: every state collapsed during COVID and rebounded sharply - but the rebound was concentrated in NSW and Victoria, which together drove most of the record post-pandemic peak.

Part 4 Conclusion

Visa class is the strongest predictor of where someone lands. NSW and Victoria are anchored by student visas, Queensland by New Zealand citizens, and Western Australia by skilled workers. Each state runs on a different visa engine, which is why the national average tells you almost nothing about what migration looks like on the ground.

Conclusion

Four Findings From Four Parts

Each part of this page contributes one finding. Together they describe how Australia's migration system actually behaves.

Pandemic disruption, not reset

Net migration collapsed to -84,940 in 2020-21 but rebounded to a record 538,340 in 2022-23. By 2024-25 it had settled at 306,000, close to the long-run trend. The swing was unprecedented but it did not change the underlying trajectory.

Origins shifted east

In 2004-05 the UK was the largest source. By 2024-25 India contributes more than three times as many migrants as the UK. South and Central Asia overtook North-West Europe as the largest region of origin around 2007 and has held that position since.

Eight distinct profiles

Raw counts make NSW and Victoria dominate. Per capita, the Northern Territory leads at three times Tasmania's rate. Each state draws on a different top-three source country mix, so no national average captures the system well.

Visa class predicts geography

Higher-education students anchor NSW and Victoria. Skilled-worker visas anchor Western Australia. New Zealand citizens anchor Queensland. The visa pathway a migrant arrives on predicts the state they land in more reliably than any other variable.
